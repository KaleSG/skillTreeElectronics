# Early Learner Content Architecture

This file contains the current working plan for the initial learning pathway expeted for implementation in revision 1.0 of skill-tree electronics

> Early learning is designed to be as linear as possible, without too much branching so new learners aren't confused as where to go next.

## Architecture

Early Learning is split into major Categories that follow a linear profession as follows:

> Skill Nodes are unmarked; Categorical Nodes are marked with "\*" at the end of it's title, which is elaborated on in the **Categorical Breakdown** section.

1. [**Welcome to SkillTree Electronics**] ->
2. [**What are electronics?**] ->
3. [**Fundamentals of Mechanics**]\* ->
4. [**Fundamentals of Electrostatics**]\* ->
5. [**Fundamentals of Circuits**]\* ->
6. [**Introduction to Components and Devices**]\*

## Node Overviews & Purpose

1. [**Welcome to SkillTree Electronics**]
   - Introduction to the website, what its purpose is, how to use it, and why it was made.

2. [**What are electronics?**]
   - Basic definitions of what electronics are, why they are so important, brief examples of different types of electronics in different industries

3. [**Fundamentals of Mechanics**]\*
   - Covers essential topics in mechanics relevant to electrical engineering. Critical to understanding voltage, current, and power at a fundamental level.
4. [**Fundamentals of Electrostatics & Electromagentism**]\*
   - Covers essential electrostatics & Electromagnetism necessary to understand how electronics actually work and give foundational understanding of what voltage, current, and power actually are. W/examples of mechanical and electromagnetic applications.
5. [**Fundamentals of Circuits**]\*
   - Fundamentals of AC and DC circuit analysis for abstract devices
   - Fundamentals of types of electronics, classifications in terms of IV curves, using abstract device examples.
   - Explicitly avoids introducing resistors, but introduces ohms law AFTER IV curves
6. [**Introduction to Components and Devices**]\*
   - Major Passive Component - Applications
   - Major Active Components
   - Introduction to ICs - Topical
